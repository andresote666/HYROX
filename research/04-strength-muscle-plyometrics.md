# 04 · Strength, leg muscle and plyometrics while losing weight

Compiled 26 Nov 2026 after Andy asked: "Are we doing enough strength and plyometrics? I don't want to lose muscle, my legs and calves are thin, and I want to avoid new injuries."

Evidence labels as in the other reports: **[EVIDENCE]** peer-reviewed, **[ANALYSIS]** my reasoning from it, **[COACH]** practitioner opinion.

## Verdict on the plan before this change

| Question | Before | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Strength training? | 2×/week heavy: squat, Bulgarian split squat, trap-bar deadlift, sled push/pull, pull-ups, farmers, soleus | **Good for strength and running economy.** Kept as it was. |

| Upper body kept in the deficit? | Pulling yes (pull-ups, sled pull, rows); pressing only optional push-ups | **Weak** for chest/shoulders. |
| Plyometrics? | Only burpee broad jumps (race practice, not progressive) | **Missing.** No graded low-level hopping. |
| Hamstring injury prevention? | Deadlift only | **Missing** knee-flexion eccentric work. |

## 1. Building leg muscle (and keeping it) in a calorie deficit

- **Volume drives growth.** Weekly sets per muscle show a graded dose-response; ≥10 sets a week beat fewer [EVIDENCE, Schoenfeld 2017 meta-analysis]. Later work suggests 12–20 sets for trained men [EVIDENCE, Baz-Valle 2022 review].
- **A deficit blunts muscle gain more than strength gain.** Across studies, lean-mass gains shrank as the deficit grew (roughly none at ~500 kcal/day), while strength gains were unaffected [EVIDENCE, Murphy & Koehler 2022 meta-regression; the exact threshold is debated].
- **High resistance-training volume (≥10 sets/muscle/week) during diets showed little or no lean-mass loss** in trained athletes [EVIDENCE, 2022 review of dieting athletes].
- **Protein**: the plan's 1.8–2.2 g/kg stays. The evidence for higher protein in lean, trained people dieting is thinner than consensus suggests (only 5 head-to-head studies) [EVIDENCE, Refalo/Helms meta-regression], but it is low risk.
- **Calves**: calf raises with partial reps in the stretched (bottom) position grew the gastrocnemius more than full-range or top-half reps [EVIDENCE, Kassiano 2023, n=42 young women, 8 weeks; one follow-up in trained people agreed weakly].

**What this means for you [ANALYSIS]:** your planned loss is slow (0.25–0.5 kg/week, ~300–500 kcal/day) and the app already adds food when you lose faster than 0.5 kg/week. So expect strength to rise and leg muscle to grow modestly during weeks 1–11, faster from week 12 when calories go to maintenance. Calves are partly genetic; 14 hard sets a week with deep-stretch reps is the best-evidenced way to move them.

## 2. Keeping muscle with less time (taper, peak)

- Strength and size are largely kept on about a third of the weekly volume **if the load stays heavy** [EVIDENCE, Bickel 2011]. Intensity is the key variable for keeping performance when frequency and volume drop [EVIDENCE, Spiering 2021 review].
- The plan already does this in weeks 13–16 (2 sets, same loads). Kept.

## 3. Plyometrics

- **Running economy**: plyometrics alone give small effects; combined with heavy strength the effect is larger, especially with >7 weeks and >2 sessions/week [EVIDENCE, meta-analysis in Kinesiology; Llanos-Lagos 2024 Sports Med]. Heavy resistance alone edged plyometrics alone [EVIDENCE, Eihara 2022].
- **Dose for stiffness**: plyometric training gives a small gain in leg stiffness; **<250 jumps/week over >7 weeks worked best**, and >500/week trended negative [EVIDENCE, Moran 2023 meta-analysis].
- **Bone**: bone responds to fast, high-magnitude, varied loads in **low** repetitions, not to many slow cycles [EVIDENCE, Warden review]. In vivo, drop jumps did **not** produce higher tibial strains or strain rates than running [EVIDENCE, Milgrom 2000]; a basketball model suggested jumping can return before full-pace running in rehab [EVIDENCE, modelling].
- **No trial** has shown that pogo hops prevent shin bone stress injuries in runners. Using them for that is **[ANALYSIS]** built on the bone-loading and stiffness evidence above.
- **Return to run after tibial bone stress**: no validated criteria; programmes are individual and symptom-led [EVIDENCE, George 2024 scoping review; Warden 2021]. Pain-free single-leg hopping is a common clinical gate (already in the morning check-in).

## 4. Injury prevention from strength work

- Strength training cut sports injuries to under a third and nearly halved overuse injuries [EVIDENCE, Lauersen 2014 meta-analysis of RCTs]; more volume/intensity, more protection [EVIDENCE, Lauersen 2018].
- Programmes including the Nordic hamstring curl cut hamstring injuries by up to ~51% (risk ratio ~0.55 in low-bias studies) [EVIDENCE, van Dyk 2019, 8,459 athletes].

## 5. What changed in the app (weeks 1–16)

| Day | Added | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Hops · 5 min** before squats; **Muscle · legs**: leg press 3×10–15 + loaded standing calf raise 3×10–15 (deep-stretch pause, then bottom half reps) | Quad and calf volume; hops + heavy lifting combo |
| Wed | Calf raise now **loaded** single-leg 3×12–15; **DB bench press 3×8–12** (was optional push-ups) | Third calf day; keep chest/shoulders in the deficit |
| Thu | **Hops · 5 min** after the run; **Nordic curl** (2×4 → 3×6), **leg curl** 3×10–15, loaded calf raise 3×10–15 | Hamstrings (injury + size), calves |
| Weeks 14–15 | Loaded calf raise kept at 2 sets | Keep calves through the taper |

Hop progression (contacts per session, Mon and Thu): 20 → 30 → 56 → 20 (deload) → 65 → 62 → 78 → 30 (deload) → 90 → 90 → 62 → 70 → 62. Two-foot pogos and line hops first, single-leg pogos from week 6. Weekly contacts including burpee broad jumps peak at 240.

Weekly hard sets in build weeks: quads ~10 (+ sleds, lunges, wall balls), hamstrings ~10, calves ~14. Deload weeks drop a set.

**Shin safety rules (automatic in the app):**
- Amber morning: hops → slow calf raises and single-leg balance.
- Red morning or the return-to-run lock: hops → isometric calf holds and balance.
- Week 1 hops only if the 10 single-leg hops in the check-in were pain-free.
- Weeks 1–2 muscle blocks start at 2 sets so sore legs don't spoil the first runs; Nordics start at 2×4.

**Trade-off [ANALYSIS]:** Mon and Thu each get ~10–15 minutes longer (Mon 75–85, Thu 85–95 min). If time is short, drop the optional accessories first, then the leg press; keep the calf raises and the hops.
